# 1. Production Intent

The learner has already completed Stages 1–3. This module should therefore feel like a **deeper look**, not another introduction to VA work.

The central experience is:

> **You have already heard that VA work comes in many forms. Now let’s look at the actual work behind the labels.**

The learner should finish with observations, not a diagnosis.

The desired feeling is:

> “I can look at a VA opportunity and understand what the work actually involves.”

Not:

> “The system told me which niche I should choose.”

## Discovery panel

### What did you notice?

You may have seen a pattern:

- One title can contain several kinds of work.
- Different titles can contain similar kinds of work.
- Some titles describe the **business area** or **system** more than the work itself.

### Key idea

> **The title is a clue. The task list tells you much more.**

You’ll use that idea throughout this module.

# 4. SECTION 2 — WHAT KIND OF WORK IS THIS?

## Section opening

### Let’s give the work a language.

You don’t need to memorize a list of VA “types.”

Instead, we’ll use five simple work patterns to describe what people actually do.

A real VA role can contain more than one pattern.

These patterns are **not personality types**. They are simply a way to look at the work.

---

# 5. Pattern Discovery Activity

## Scenario 1 — The Client’s Shared Drive

A client sends you several folders of documents. Some files are duplicated, some have unclear names, and others are in the wrong folders. Your task is to clean them up and arrange them so the team can find what they need.

### Ask

**What kind of work is happening here?**

Possible patterns:

- ORGANIZE
- COMMUNICATE
- RESEARCH
- CREATE
- OPERATE

After selection, reveal:

### Mainly: ORGANIZE

> The main job is arranging information so other people can find and use it easily.

**Think of ORGANIZE as:**

> Making information orderly and usable.

Optional reaction:

**Would you want to explore this kind of work?**

- Want to explore
- Not sure yet
- Probably not for me

This reaction is optional.

*Scenario titles are deliberately neutral: they describe the situation, never the pattern. The same question and optional reaction follow every scenario.*

---

## Scenario 2 — A Question About an Order

A customer sends a question about an order. You check the support guidelines, respond clearly, document the interaction, and follow up when the issue needs another team’s help.

### Mainly: COMMUNICATE

> Communication is central because the work helps information move clearly between the customer and the business. It also touches ORGANIZE and OPERATE because the interaction must be documented and the support process followed.

**Think of COMMUNICATE as:**

> Helping information move clearly between people.

---

## Scenario 3 — Several Competitors

A client wants to compare several competitors. You gather information from their websites, organize the findings, compare prices and product features, and highlight changes that may matter to the client.

### Mainly: RESEARCH

> The work involves finding, checking, comparing, and analyzing information so the client can make a decision.

**Think of RESEARCH as:**

> Finding and making sense of information that helps someone act.

Numbers can be part of this work. Basic reporting and noticing changes in business numbers also belong here.

---

## Scenario 4 — Next Month’s Topics

A client gives you next month’s content topics. You prepare simple graphics, format captions, organize the content calendar, and prepare the files for review.

### Mainly: CREATE

> Producing the content is the main task. The work also involves ORGANIZE because the materials need to be arranged and prepared properly.

**Think of CREATE as:**

> Producing useful content or visual material.

---

## Scenario 5 — Orders Throughout the Day

A client receives online orders throughout the day. You process the orders in the store system, check inventory information, update order statuses, and flag problems that need attention.

### Mainly: OPERATE

> The main job is keeping an ongoing business process moving correctly inside the tools the business uses.

**Think of OPERATE as:**

> Keeping a recurring business process running inside its systems.

### A useful distinction

> **ORGANIZE** makes information orderly and usable.
>
> **OPERATE** keeps an ongoing business process working correctly.

For example:

- Cleaning up and arranging an order spreadsheet → **ORGANIZE**
- Processing and updating orders in the store system → **OPERATE**

The point is not to find a perfect label. The point is to explain **why** one pattern is more central to the work.

---

# 6. Pattern Reference Panel

After the scenarios, provide a compact reference rather than a long lesson.

## ORGANIZE
Arranging information so people can find, understand, and use it.

## COMMUNICATE
Helping information move clearly between people.

## RESEARCH
Finding, checking, comparing, and analyzing information so someone can make a decision or take action.

## CREATE
Producing useful content or visual material.

## OPERATE
Keeping a recurring business process running inside the tools and systems used by the business.

### Remember

> These are work patterns, not labels for people.

A single VA role can involve several of them.

## CARD 01 — GENERAL VA / ADMINISTRATIVE SUPPORT

### What does the work involve?

Helping a business stay organized by handling recurring administrative tasks and keeping information, schedules, documents, and follow-ups moving.

### Examples of tasks

- Organizing files and records
- Updating spreadsheets or trackers
- Managing calendars and appointments
- Preparing documents
- Following up on outstanding items
- Maintaining simple reports
- Coordinating routine administrative tasks

### Work patterns commonly involved

**ORGANIZE · COMMUNICATE · RESEARCH · OPERATE**

### What kind of work environment?

Often a mix of internal coordination and independent task work. Some work is async; some requires specific working hours.

### What can be demanding about it?

The work can involve many different tasks in one role. Staying organized while switching between priorities is important.

### Typical path

**Often entered directly**

This is a common starting direction, especially when a learner already has transferable administrative experience.

### Useful skills

Organization, written communication, attention to detail, basic spreadsheets, file management, and following instructions.

### What would you need to learn?

The specific tools and workflows used by the client.

---

## CARD 02 — CUSTOMER SUPPORT VA

### What does the work involve?

Helping customers through email, chat, tickets, or other support channels while following the business’s support process.

### Examples of tasks

- Answering customer questions
- Handling support tickets
- Following support guidelines
- Documenting customer interactions
- Escalating issues
- Following up on unresolved concerns
- Updating customer records

### Work patterns commonly involved

**COMMUNICATE · ORGANIZE · OPERATE**

### What kind of work environment?

Often customer-facing. Some roles require live or scheduled coverage; others are primarily async.

### What can be demanding about it?

Customer concerns can be repetitive, urgent, or emotionally difficult. Clear communication and consistency matter.

### Typical path

**Often entered directly**

Customer service or BPO experience can be relevant, depending on the role.

### Useful skills

Written communication, patience, listening, problem handling, documentation, and following procedures.

### What would you need to learn?

The client’s products, support policies, ticketing system, and escalation process.

---

## CARD 03 — E-COMMERCE VA

System-heavy and detail-oriented. Responsibilities vary significantly between clients.

## CARD 06 — SOCIAL MEDIA / CONTENT SUPPORT

### What does the work involve?

Helping a business plan, prepare, organize, and publish content for social platforms.

## CARD 09 — LEAD GENERATION / APPOINTMENT SETTING

## CARD 10 — TECHNICAL / SPECIALIZED VA SUPPORT

## CARD 11 — NDIS VA

### What does the work involve?

Supporting Australian disability service providers that work under the National Disability Insurance Scheme (NDIS) with administrative, scheduling, documentation, and claims-related tasks.

### Examples of tasks

- Maintaining participant and service records
- Scheduling and rostering support workers
- Preparing and organizing service documents
- Supporting administrative tasks related to invoicing and client records
- Following up with participants, families, or support workers
- Keeping records accurate for compliance checks
- Updating the provider’s client management system

### Work patterns commonly involved

**ORGANIZE · OPERATE · COMMUNICATE**

### What kind of work environment?

Usually admin-heavy and process-driven, inside the provider’s systems. Work may follow Australian business hours, depending on the client and role.

### What can be demanding about it?

Participant information is private and sensitive, and the scheme has detailed rules for documentation and claims. Accuracy and careful handling of information are important.

### Typical path

**Often entered with related experience**

Administrative, healthcare, or disability-support experience can be useful preparation.

### Useful skills

Organization, attention to detail, written communication, confidentiality, and following procedures.

### What would you need to learn?

NDIS terminology, the provider’s systems and procedures, the claims process, and privacy and documentation requirements.

## Reaction control

The same optional reaction appears after every scenario explanation and at the end of every Direction Card:

> **Would you want to explore this kind of work?**

- Want to explore → stored as `explore`
- Not sure yet → stored as `unsure`
- Probably not for me → stored as `not_for_me`

Under each control: *This reaction is optional.* The learner can continue without choosing, and can change or remove a reaction later in the Clue Panel.

## Clue Panel

### WANT TO EXPLORE
Work or directions that caught your attention.

### NOT SURE YET
Work or directions you need more information or experience to judge.

### PROBABLY NOT FOR ME
Work you do not currently want to explore.

Each item may have an optional note:

> **Why did you react this way?**

Keep the note optional.

Allow the learner to change a reaction later.

# 13. Data / Interaction Requirements

## Single source of truth

Use `workClues` for all learner reactions.

Do not separately persist `interestedDirections`.

A direction can be derived from clues where:

`sourceType = direction` and `reaction = explore`

Likewise, scenario reactions can remain part of the same structure.

## Suggested structure

```text
stage4Profile
├── schemaVersion
├── workClues[]
│   ├── id
│   ├── sourceType: "scenario" | "direction"
│   ├── sourceId
│   ├── reaction: "explore" | "unsure" | "not_for_me"
│   ├── note
│   └── updatedAt
├── scenarioObservations[]       { scenarioId, selectedPatterns[], completed }
├── jobPostObservations[]        { postId, selected[], completed }
├── exploredDirections[]
└── modules                      { "4-1": { lastSection, directionsReviewed, completedAt } }
```

Reaction values are exactly `explore`, `unsure`, `not_for_me`.

`exploredDirections` may record that a learner opened or viewed a card, but it must never determine completion or produce recommendations.

## Persistence

- Browser/local persistence
- Export
- Restore
- Stable IDs
- `schemaVersion`

The schema must be forward-compatible enough that an export created while only 4.1 exists can still be restored after 4.2–4.4 are added.

---

# 14. Completion Logic

Completion should require evidence of actual engagement:

- job-post activity completed;
- pattern scenarios completed;
- the learner has moved through the direction overview (pressed Continue at the end of Explore VA Directions), not opened every card;
- at least one Work Clue saved.

Do not require opening every individual card.

Do not require a specific reaction.

Do not calculate a score.

Do not generate a recommended direction.

Do not block completion because the learner selected “probably not for me.”

---

# 15. Visual / UX Direction

The module should feel like an interactive field guide to VA work rather than a test.

Use:

- clear section progression;
- generous readable cards;
- compact pattern references;
- concrete task examples;
- neutral reaction controls;
- persistent Work Clues access;
- mobile-first card behavior.

Avoid:

- personality-test visuals;
- score meters;
- ranking bars;
- “top match” cards;
- badges that look like achievements or identity labels;
- excessive animation;
- decorative interaction with no learning purpose.

The learner should always understand **why** an interaction exists.

- All learner state uses the shared Stage 4 architecture.

# 17. Production Principle

Do not add content merely because a course normally has another lesson, quiz, reflection, badge, or summary.

Every screen should earn its place by helping the learner answer one question:

> **What kind of VA work is this, and how do I feel about exploring it?**

The learner is not choosing a permanent career.

They are becoming better at choosing **what to explore next**.
