# 1. Purpose

Module 4.2 is the **ME** part of Stage 4.

Module 4.1 asked:

> **What kinds of VA work are there?**

Module 4.2 turns the learner's attention inward:

> **What do I already bring to the table?**

The purpose is to help learners recognize work they have **actually done** — including work that did not come from a VA job — and see honestly how much experience they have with it.

The learner should leave with a clearer picture of:

- what they have already done;
- what that work actually involved;
- how much experience they have with each kind of task;
- what they have only seen, or not done yet.

This is **self-understanding from evidence**, not career diagnosis, and not matching.

---

# 2. Design Principle

## Experience first. Labels second.

Do not begin with abstract questions such as:

> "What are your strengths?"

Begin with concrete experience:

> "What have you actually done?"

Then help the learner look at what that experience involved.

For example:

> "I handled customer complaints in a BPO."

can become concrete tasks such as:

- answered customer questions by chat and email;
- documented each interaction;
- followed an escalation process;
- updated customer records in a system.

The learner should discover these things in their own experience rather than be assigned a label.

---

# 3. Relationship to Earlier Stages and 4.1

## 3.1 Stage 1 → 4.2 boundary (new)

VA Foundations **Lesson 3, "Who Can Become a VA?"** already teaches that:

- previous experience can transfer into VA work;
- tasks matter more than job titles;
- non-traditional backgrounds count;
- and it asks learners to self-check six general skills ("Yes · Some · Need Practice").

**Boundary:**

- **Stage 1 tells** learners their background can count, and has them self-check general skills.
- **4.2 has them produce evidence** from their own specific experiences: concrete tasks, how much they have done them, and what that work involved.

4.2 must not reteach the concept of transferable skills, and must not reuse Lesson 3's three-level self-check or its wording. The opening may acknowledge Stage 1 once, as 4.1 acknowledges Stage 1 Lesson 6.

## 3.2 4.1 → 4.2

4.1 and 4.2 are two halves of one investigation.

### 4.1 — WORK
Job descriptions, work scenarios, the five work patterns, VA directions, and the learner's reactions to actual work (Work Clues).

### 4.2 — ME
The learner's own experience, the tasks they actually performed, how much they have done them, and what that work involved (described with the same five patterns).

> **Look at the work → look at what you have actually done → compare the two in 4.3.**

4.2 **uses** the five patterns as a shared language (as tags on the learner's tasks). It does not teach the patterns again; 4.1 already ends with a Pattern Reference Panel.

## 3.3 4.2 → 4.3

4.2 does **not** perform matching. It gives 4.3 two independent inputs:

- **what interests me** (Work Clues from 4.1);
- **what I have actually done** (evidence from 4.2).

4.3 is where those come together.

# 4. Audience

The learner has completed Stages 1–3 and 4.1.

They already understand basic digital skills, professional communication, following and checking instructions, the range of VA work, and that titles can hide very different work.

4.2 should **not** feel like a beginner lesson about transferable skills. It should feel like evidence-gathering for someone who is already becoming work-ready.

---

# 5. Filipino Learner Context

Use Filipino context to make examples recognizable, not to lower the learning level.

Relevant experience may come from:

- BPO/customer service;
- office or administrative work;
- teaching;
- healthcare;
- sales;
- accounting/bookkeeping;
- retail or hospitality;
- online selling;
- family or small-business work;
- freelancing;
- content/social media work;
- technical or IT-related work;
- community or organizational responsibilities;
- school, internship or fresh-graduate experience;
- previous VA work.

The module should communicate that useful experience can come from many places, while making clear that **not every experience is automatically relevant** — the point is to look closely at what was actually done.

A learner should not need a previous VA job title to complete 4.2.

---

# 6. Learning Outcomes

By the end of 4.2, the learner should be able to:

1. identify concrete experiences they can draw from, including non-employment experience;
2. describe what they actually did in those experiences, as tasks rather than titles;
3. state honestly how much experience they have with each task;
4. describe what that work involved, using the five work patterns from 4.1;
5. distinguish **capabilities that can carry across contexts** (for example, documenting interactions accurately) from **knowledge tied to a particular tool, role or industry** (for example, a specific ticketing system or Seller Central);
6. recognize what they have only seen, or have not done yet, without treating it as a failing;
7. carry this evidence forward into 4.3.

Not outcomes of 4.2:

- identify a niche, direction or "best fit";
- discover a personality type or strengths profile;
- calculate readiness;
- identify what to learn for a particular direction (that is 4.3).

---

# 7. Core Learning Model

> **EXPERIENCE → TASKS → EVIDENCE → WHAT THE WORK INVOLVED**

### EXPERIENCE
Where does this experience come from?

### TASKS
What did you actually do?

### EVIDENCE
How much have you actually done this? (see §9)

### WHAT THE WORK INVOLVED
Which kinds of work did that involve? Described with the five 4.1 patterns, as tags on each task.

**Transfer**, in 4.2, means only this: *the kind of work you did there (for example, keeping records accurate inside a system) is the same kind of work 4.1 described as OPERATE.* The learner sees that experience is portable **without being told where to take it**.

**Removed from 4.2:**

- TRANSFER as a mapping to VA directions → 4.3.
- GAPS / "Need to Build" → 4.3, measured against the directions the learner chooses to examine.

# 8. Learner Flow

The interaction design remains open for production design; this is the intended shape.

## Section 1 — Start With Your Experience

Prompt along the lines of:

> **Before VA work, what kinds of work or responsibilities have you already handled?**

Allow several sources rather than one career history. Source categories (prompts, not identity categories):

- Work experience
- Business or selling experience
- School or internship experience
- Freelance experience
- Volunteer/community experience
- Family or personal responsibilities
- Previous VA experience
- Other

Ask **what the experience was**, not where or for whom. Employer, client or family names are not needed (see §15).

## Section 2 — What Did You Actually Do?

Move from the experience to concrete tasks.

Instead of:

> "I worked in a BPO."

Ask:

> **"What did you actually do there?"**

The strongest task-gathering prompt is:

> **"What did people rely on you to handle?"**

Use it to surface concrete tasks from the chosen experience.

Example tasks (illustrative):

- answered customer questions;
- documented interactions;
- followed a support process;
- updated records;
- prepared reports;
- handled schedules;
- escalated issues;
- monitored orders;
- coordinated with other people.

A **task record** means a task the learner has deliberately chosen to examine for that experience, either from a short prompt or in the learner’s own words. The learner is not asked to answer every possible task prompt.

**Unpicked prompts are never stored as tasks.** Therefore, “I have not done this yet” does not mean “the learner skipped a prompt.” It means the learner deliberately chose to examine that task and honestly reported that they have not done it yet.

Keep task prompts **short and specific to the chosen experience**, so learners describe what they did rather than tick a long checklist.

Everyday-task prompts belong inside relevant experience sources rather than in a separate low-experience mode. For example, family/personal, school/internship, volunteer/community, and Other can surface prompts such as keeping track of receipts, organizing an activity, handling group updates, maintaining a schedule, comparing options, or making simple materials.

## Section 3 — How Much Have You Done It?

For each task the learner has chosen to examine, the learner gives one **evidence answer** from the four named answers in §9.

The answer describes experience, not ability.

## Section 4 — What Did That Work Involve?

The learner chooses which of the five 4.1 patterns describe each task:

- ORGANIZE
- COMMUNICATE
- RESEARCH
- CREATE
- OPERATE

The learner makes the judgment. The system does **not** classify free-text tasks by keywords or automatically assign tags.

For prompted tasks, a reference explanation or reference tag may be shown **after** the learner chooses, as a learning aid. It is not a right/wrong result. Free-text tasks receive no system-generated reference tag.

Rules:

- Patterns are **tags on individual tasks**, never headings that collect the learner’s evidence.
- **No counts, totals, percentages or ordering by pattern** for this learner.
- Never phrase as “You are an ORGANIZE person.” Phrase as “This task involved ORGANIZE.”
- Any task may have more than one pattern tag when the learner sees more than one kind of work in it.

This section is also where the learner distinguishes **capabilities that can carry across contexts** from **knowledge tied to a particular tool, role or industry**.

Add one concrete interaction after the learner tags a task that names a tool or system:

> **Would this kind of task still make sense if the tool or system were different?**

The learner sees the distinction between the work itself and knowledge of a particular tool. This is an understanding activity, not a score or classification.

## Section 5 — My Evidence Snapshot

The three-part artifact (§14), shown to the learner and editable.

The snapshot uses mutually exclusive task evidence for Parts 1 and 3. Part 2 is an interpretation view of the work already recorded; it is not a second task list:

1. **What I’ve done** — experiences and tasks answered “I do this regularly” or “I have done this before”.
2. **What that work involved** — the learner’s pattern observations, shown alongside the relevant task context from the recorded evidence. This is a view of the existing task records, not an additional copy of the tasks in stored data.
3. **What I haven’t done yet or have only seen** — tasks answered “I have some exposure to it” or “I have not done this yet”.

A task therefore belongs to either Part 1 or Part 3 according to its evidence answer. Part 2 does not create another stored task record or a duplicate evidence grouping.

## Section 6 — Your Work Clues Come With You

At the end, the learner’s Work Clues from 4.1 appear **read-only**, framed as:

> You also have these Work Clues from 4.1. You’ll bring them, and your evidence, into 4.3.

If there are no Work Clues:

> **You don’t have any Work Clues from 4.1 yet.** You can add them in 4.1, but you can still continue here.

No connection is drawn between a Work Clue and any experience. That comparison is 4.3.

# 9. Evidence, Not Self-Rating

Do not ask learners to rate themselves (1–10; beginner/intermediate/expert; weak/average/strong; percentages; confidence scores).

Ask for evidence:

> **How much experience do you actually have with this task?**

These are **evidence answers**, not levels:

- **I do this regularly** — this was a recurring part of what I did, now or in the past.
- **I have done this before** — I have personally carried out this task, but it was not a recurring part of what I did.
- **I have some exposure to it** — I have encountered, observed, assisted with, or had limited hands-on experience with this task, but I have not independently done it as a completed task.
- **I have not done this yet** — I have not personally carried out this task.

### Rules

These answers describe **experience evidence, not ability**.

They are stored as named string values, never as numbers.

Nothing may sort, average, score, compare, rank, or otherwise treat one evidence answer as better or worse than another. The only permitted grouping in 4.2 is the mutually exclusive snapshot grouping defined in §8.

Copy, layout and data must never present the four answers as a competence meter:

- no “level” numbers;
- no progress bars or fill-meters;
- no colors that imply better/worse;
- no totals;
- no sorting by evidence answer.

“I have not done this yet” is a complete, honest answer, not a failure state.

# 10. What the Work Involved (replaces v0.1 "Transfer Activity")

After the learner has recorded tasks and evidence, show that **the same kind of work appears in many contexts**, using only the five patterns.

Example:

> **Experience:** Online selling (own store)  
> **Task:** Updated product details and stock counts in the store system — *I do this regularly*  
> **What it involved:** OPERATE, ORGANIZE

The learner can see that this is the kind of work 4.1 called OPERATE. **No VA direction names appear** in connection with the learner's own evidence. Which directions that might connect to is the first question of 4.3.

---

# 11. Experience That Learners May Overlook

Keep from v0.1. One important purpose of 4.2 is to surface experience learners may dismiss because it did not come from formal employment:

- **Online selling:** handled customer messages, maintained product information, processed orders, tracked inventory, coordinated deliveries.
- **Family business:** maintained records, handled suppliers, followed up with customers, scheduled activities, prepared simple reports.
- **Teaching:** organized materials, communicated instructions, tracked records, prepared documents, coordinated schedules.
- **BPO:** handled customer communication, followed workflows, documented interactions, used business systems, escalated issues.

The goal is not to tell the learner every experience is valuable. It is to help them **look closely enough to see what they actually did**.

---

# 12. Capabilities vs Role-Specific Knowledge

Keep from v0.1 — this is genuinely new learning in Stage 4.

### Capabilities that can carry across contexts
Written communication, organization, research, documentation, follow-up, spreadsheet use, customer handling, process-following, coordination.

### Knowledge tied to a particular tool, role or industry
A specific accounting system, Amazon Seller Central, a healthcare workflow, a CRM, NDIS terminology and provider procedures, a client's internal process.

In 4.2 this distinction is taught **through the learner's own tasks** (§8, Section 4). It prepares 4.3, where the learner compares their evidence with what a direction would require.

---

# 13. Experience Across Different Backgrounds

4.2 must work for learners with little formal work history: fresh graduates, students, parents, and people whose experience is mostly family or community.

There is **no separate low-experience path or mode**.

Instead, everyday-task prompts are offered naturally within relevant experience sources, so all learners can use them when appropriate. Examples include:

- kept track of money or receipts;
- organized an event or activity;
- handled messages or updates for a group;
- kept a schedule for other people;
- looked up and compared options before a purchase or decision;
- made posters, posts or simple materials.

“I have some exposure to it” and “I have not done this yet” are valid evidence answers, and a snapshot containing them is a complete, honest result.

No minimum number of experiences or tasks is required.

Keep task prompts short. A long checklist invites ticking everything, which undermines evidence.

If a learner wants to make an evidence note more concrete, an optional short prompt may ask:

> **When did you do this, and how often?**

Do not ask where it was done or for whom.

# 14. Learner Artifact — "What I Bring to the Table"

Three parts, editable, not a résumé:

### 1. What I've done
My experiences and the concrete tasks I did in each.

### 2. What that work involved
A derived interpretation view of the recorded tasks, showing the learner’s pattern observations alongside the relevant task context and evidence answer. This is not a duplicate task list.

### 3. What I haven't done yet or have only seen
Tasks marked "I have some exposure to it" or "I have not done this yet".

Rules:

- Grouping is derived from the evidence answers.
- No counts, totals, percentages, rankings or per-pattern summaries.
- No direction names.
- No "What may transfer" or "What I still need to build" sections (both are 4.3).

---

# 15. Personal Data (new)

- Ask **what was done**, not where or for whom.
- Tell learners they don't need to enter names of employers, clients, schools or family members.
- Notes are optional and short.
- Do not collect health, financial, family or identity details.
- Do not ask where an experience took place or for whom it was done.
- Data stays in the learner's browser only, with Export/Restore, as in 4.1.

---

# 16. Connection to Work Clues

4.1 creates `workClues`. 4.2:

- does **not** read Work Clues during evidence gathering;
- does **not** filter, order or prompt experiences based on Work Clues;
- shows Work Clues **read-only at the end** as a handoff to 4.3;
- never states or implies a link between a Work Clue and the learner's experience.

The learner should never see: "Because you chose these clues, you are suited for X", or "Let's find experience that supports the work you liked."

# 17. State Architecture Constraints

The implementation schema is **not finalized** in this brief; it will be set in the 4.2 Build Brief. These constraints are locked at design level:

1. **One store for experience.** A single list of experience records. Each record has a stable ID, a source (one of the §8 categories), an optional short note, and its tasks.
2. **Tasks hold the evidence.** Each task has a stable ID, a label (from a prompt, or the learner's own short text), zero or more pattern tags chosen by the learner (the five 4.1 pattern IDs), and **exactly one named evidence-answer value** from the four evidence answers.
3. **Derived, not stored:**
   - snapshot grouping by evidence answer;
   - display views derived from the learner’s task records;
   - anything resembling transfer notes or development needs (removed from 4.2).
4. **No counts, totals, per-pattern aggregates, sorting, averaging or comparison of evidence answers** are stored or displayed.
5. **Fits the live Stage 4 application.** 4.2 shares the existing Stage 4 state (`pva-stage4-progress`). The Build Brief must specify:
   - a schema version increment with a migration step, so v1 data and v1 backups still load and restore;
   - per-module progress entries (4.1's `directionsReviewed` stays 4.1-only; 4.2 gets its own completion fields);
   - field-by-field validation of the new data on load and Restore, as 4.1 does now.
6. **Single source of truth preserved.** `workClues` remains the only record of reactions; 4.2 does not copy or reinterpret it.
7. **Editable and removable.** The learner can edit or delete any experience or task. Deleting an experience also deletes all tasks belonging to it; deleting a task removes its evidence and pattern tags. No orphan task data remains.
8. **Completion persistence.** Once 4.2 completion is reached, it remains reached even if the learner later edits or deletes evidence. The learner can continue editing the artifact without being unexpectedly marked incomplete.
9. **Minimal personal data** (§15).

These constraints leave 4.3 exactly what it needs:

- the learner's own evidence per task, with pattern tags;
- Work Clues;
- the 4.1 Direction Card fields, including "What would you need to learn?".

4.2 pre-computes none of the matching.

# 18. Completion (new)

Completion rewards meaningful engagement, not data volume.

A learner completes 4.2 when they have:

1. recorded **at least one experience** (any source, including school, family, community or “Other”);
2. described or selected **at least one concrete task** for it;
3. given an **evidence answer** for each of its tasks;
4. made **at least one learner-made pattern-tag choice** for a task. Any valid tag choice counts; there is no minimum number of tags and no right or wrong tag set;
5. **reached the snapshot** (Section 5).

Do not require:

- a minimum number of experiences or tasks;
- a particular source or background;
- a particular evidence answer;
- any 4.1 reaction or Work Clue;
- a direction or a score.

# 19. Reflection Design

There is no separate reflection step.

Use concrete prompts inside Section 2 to help the learner surface tasks, especially:

> **“What did people rely on you to handle?”**

Other useful prompts may be used there:

> “Which tasks have you handled repeatedly?”

> “What did you have to keep track of, communicate, look up, make, or keep running?”

Avoid “What are your strengths?” and similar abstract prompts.

# 20. Three Traps

## Résumé trap
4.2 is not a résumé builder. No résumé templates, professional summaries, application statements, portfolio descriptions, LinkedIn copy or interview answers.

## Personality-test trap
No personality types, archetypes, strengths profiles, color types, aptitude scores, hidden scoring, or "you are naturally…" statements. Patterns are tags on tasks, never labels for the learner. No per-pattern tallies.

## Matching trap
No top directions, recommended niches, best matches, compatibility percentages, rankings, or "you should explore X". **4.2 may name work patterns and tasks. It may not name VA directions in connection with the learner's own evidence.**

# 23. Visual / Tone Direction

Practical, valuable, authentic, calm, approachable, learner-centered.

The learner should feel they are **examining their own evidence**, not taking a test.

Avoid:

- motivational-poster language;
- career-coach imagery;
- personality-test aesthetics;
- badges, score meters and excessive animation.

---

# 24. Boundaries

4.2 is about recognizing what the learner has actually done.

It is NOT: VA skills training, niche training, portfolio building, résumé writing, job applications, interview preparation, client positioning, salary optimization, market ranking, personality or aptitude testing, career diagnosis, matching, or a recommendation engine.

# 25. What 4.2 Discovers / What Waits for 4.3

### Discovered in 4.2

- What I have actually done.
- What that work involved (pattern tags).
- How much I have done it (evidence).
- What I have only seen or not done yet.
- That capabilities and tool/role-specific knowledge are different things.

### Deliberately left for 4.3

- Which directions my evidence connects to.
- What I would need to learn for a given direction.
- How my Work Clues (interest) compare with my evidence (experience).
- Any shortlist of directions to explore.

# 29. v0.3 Targeted Revision Check

The second peer review identified five Must Fix items. v0.3 closes them as follows:

1. **Learner chooses pattern tags.** Pattern judgment is now an explicit learner action. Reference tags, when used, appear only after the learner chooses and only for prompted tasks.
2. **Task meaning is explicit.** A task is deliberately chosen for examination. Unpicked prompts are never stored. Snapshot parts are mutually exclusive.

5. **Completion includes the main learning action.** At least one learner-made pattern-tag choice is required.

The Should Fix items are also incorporated:
- no “where” prompt;
- no separate reflection section;
- defined cascade deletion, persistent completion and Work Clues empty state;
- everyday-task prompts are integrated into relevant experience sources.

No architectural restructuring was introduced.

# 30. Definition of Success

Module 4.2 succeeds if a learner can honestly say:

> **"I can now see what I have actually done, and what that work involved."**

More specifically:

> "These are things I have actually done."  
> "This is how much I have done them."  
> "This is the kind of work they involved."  
> "These are things I have only seen, or haven't done yet."

And then be ready for the next question:

> **"Now, where do the work I'm interested in and the work I've actually done connect?"**

That is the doorway into **4.3 — Exploring Possible Directions**.
